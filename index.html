TIER TWO  ·  PART I  ·  INFORMATION

What is tier two, anyway?

Cities outside the metro headline — but inside the new growth corridor. Population 5L–20L. Already on national infrastructure. Already absorbing capital. Just not yet priced for it.

This is where India's middle is moving. It is not an idea. It is a census.

FIG. CS-001 · I  ·  THE EDITORIAL CASE

Bahadurgarh is the closest Tier Two city to the capital. Twenty-eight kilometres from Connaught Place, on a metro line that runs without a transfer, sitting on the throat of two of north India's most consequential road projects — the NH-10 and the KMP Expressway.

Population has crossed six lakhs. Industrial land has compounded thirty-five times since 2006 — not because the city was discovered, but because the corridor was funded. The infrastructure arrived first. The price followed.

Our position is the obvious one: the next decade of NCR growth does not happen in NCR. It happens at its edge. We are at the edge.

CASE I  ·  INFRASTRUCTURE LOCK

The corridor is already capex-funded.

NH-10 is widened. KMP is operational. Metro is live. RRTS is in approvals. None of these decisions are being re-litigated — the spend is locked, and the land along it has nowhere to do but absorb that decision.

02

Plotted, freehold,
documented.

Bahadurgarh's product mix is dominated by apartments and built-flat. Plotted developments with clean title at this corridor distance are structurally rare. We are positioning into the underbought slice.

Comparable corridor cities — Manesar, Sonipat — are already trading at a premium. Bahadurgarh's per-square-foot metro-adjusted price still sits at a discount. That discount is not a discount on quality. It is a discount on attention.

FIG. CS-002 · I  ·  THE EDITORIAL CASE

Founded in 1887 by Lord Lansdowne, then Viceroy. Set at 1706 metres in the Pauri Garhwal hills, four hours from Delhi NCR by road. A military cantonment town — protected by zoning, not by fashion.

Lansdowne is not Mussoorie. The cantonment status caps construction, the topology limits roads, and the demographic skews quiet. The view does not have a billboard in front of it.

For the holiday home thesis — tourism, rental, view, appreciation, second home, infrastructure — Lansdowne is the rare hill that scores on all six.

FIG. CS-002 · II  ·  SIX PILLARS · DEEP DIVE

Six pillars,
one hill.

I
PILLAR · I

Tourism that does not crowd.

Year-round footfall, but capped supply. The cantonment status keeps the town orderly and the experience differentiated — the asset is not a fight for the same week in May.

II
PILLAR · II

Rental that compounds calmly.

Short-stay yield through the calendar — without resort overheads. Premium per-night, against fixed restricted supply. The math of scarcity, not the math of volume.

III
PILLAR · III

The view is the asset.

Unobstructed Himalayan ridge — the part you cannot replicate or commoditise. In a world of sameness, the only thing you actually own is what you can see.

IV
PILLAR · IV

Appreciation — protected, not promoted.

Hill plots in cantonment-zoned regions don't see speculative supply spikes. They see slow, structural compounding — the boring kind that finishes the decade quietly ahead.

V
PILLAR · V

The second home, four hours out.

Close enough for a weekend, far enough that the weather changes. The lifestyle pillar is not romantic — it is logistical. And the logistics work.

VI
PILLAR · VI

Infrastructure that actually arrived.

All-season highway, telecom redundancy, cantonment civic grid, hospital corridor. The hill that does not improvise when it rains.
